What does a notary do?

A notary is a qualified lawyer in the third and oldest branch of the legal profession in England and Wales. Most of the work is preparing and certifying documents that will be used abroad: powers of attorney, company papers, declarations, copies of passports and certificates.

Notaries are appointed by the Court of Faculties of the Archbishop of Canterbury and regulated by the Master of the Faculties. They must be fully insured, keep client money separate, and renew their practising certificate every year.

More from the Notaries Society and the Faculty Office.

Before you book

General guidance. We will confirm what your document needs when you call.

  1. The document itself, unsigned if it needs to be signed in front of the notary.
  2. Your passport or other photo ID, and proof of address.
  3. Which country the document is going to, and who will receive it.
  4. Any instructions from the receiving authority, such as whether an apostille or e-apostille is accepted.
